1. Parts of Speech
Parts of speech classify words by the job they perform in a sentence. A word can change
category depending on use, so identify function in context rather than memorizing a single
label.

What You Must Know
   Noun: names a person, place, thing, idea, quality, or activity. Types include proper, common, collective,
    abstract, material, countable, and uncountable nouns.
   Pronoun: replaces or points to a noun. Major types: personal, possessive, reflexive, demonstrative,
    interrogative, relative, indefinite, and reciprocal.
   Verb: expresses action, occurrence, or state. Distinguish main verbs, auxiliary verbs, modal auxiliaries,
    finite verbs, non-finite forms, transitive verbs, and intransitive verbs.
   Adjective: modifies a noun or pronoun. It may describe quality, quantity, number, possession, or identity.
    Participles can function adjectivally: a broken window; a smiling child.
   Adverb: modifies a verb, adjective, another adverb, or sometimes a whole clause. Common classes
    include manner, place, time, frequency, degree, and viewpoint.
   Preposition: shows relationship, usually between a noun phrase and another element: in the room, after
    lunch, with courage.
   Conjunction: joins words, phrases, or clauses. Coordinating: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so. Subordinating:
    because, although, if, when, unless, while, etc.
   Interjection: expresses a sudden reaction or feeling: Alas!, Hurrah!, Oh!, Bravo! Articles/determiners (a, an,
    the, this, each, some) limit or specify nouns.

    Common trap: Do not classify a word by spelling alone. "Fast" can be an adjective (a fast car) or
    adverb (drive fast). "Running" can be a verb, adjective, or gerund depending on its function.

2. Tenses
English tense questions test time reference, aspect, sequence of events, signal words, and
consistency. The essential distinction is not simply past/present/future; you must also
know simple, continuous, perfect, and perfect continuous forms.

What You Must Know
     Simple present: base verb / s-form. Uses: habits, routines, facts, timetables, universal truths. Example: He
      trains daily.
     Present continuous: am/is/are + -ing. Uses: action happening now, temporary situations, arranged near
      future. Stative verbs such as know, believe, own, belong are not normally used continuously.
     Present perfect: has/have + past participle. Uses: past action linked to present, life experience, unfinished
      time, action with present result. Avoid it with finished time expressions such as yesterday or last year.
     Present perfect continuous: has/have been + -ing. Emphasizes duration or repeated ongoing activity: She
      has been studying for two hours.
     Simple past: past form. Completed events at a definite past time. Past continuous: was/were + -ing for
      background or interrupted past actions.
     Past perfect: had + past participle. Use for the earlier of two past events when sequence needs
      clarification. Past perfect continuous emphasizes duration before a past point.
     Future: will + base for predictions/spontaneous decisions; be going to for prior plans or evidence-based
      predictions; future continuous for action in progress at a future time; future perfect for completion before a
      future deadline.
     Conditionals: zero = facts; first = real future possibility; second = unreal/hypothetical present-future; third =
      unreal past. Watch verb combinations carefully.

       Common trap: Signal words help but do not replace meaning. "Since" marks a starting point; "for"
       marks duration. "By" often points to completion before a deadline and may trigger a perfect tense.



Tense Formula Snapshot
    Tense                                 Core form                               Typical use
    Present simple                        V1 / V1+s                               habit, fact
    Present continuous                    am/is/are + V-ing                       action now/temporary
    Present perfect                       has/have + V3                           past linked to present
    Past simple                           V2                                      finished past
    Past continuous                       was/were + V-ing                        past action in progress
    Past perfect                          had + V3                                earlier past action
    Future simple                         will + V1                               prediction/decision
    Future perfect                        will have + V3                          completed before future time

3. Articles
Articles are determined mainly by sound, definiteness, countability, and whether the noun
is being used specifically or generically.

What You Must Know
   Use a before a consonant sound: a university, a European, a one-time event. Use an before a vowel
    sound: an hour, an honest man, an MBA.
   Use the for something specific or already known, unique objects, superlatives, ordinal numbers, rivers,
    seas, oceans, deserts, mountain ranges, many newspapers, and certain country names such as the United
    Kingdom.
   Use no article with plural or uncountable nouns when speaking generally: Books are useful. Honesty
    matters.
   No article is normally used with languages, academic subjects, sports, continents, most countries, cities,
    streets, single mountains, and lakes: Urdu, mathematics, cricket, Asia, Pakistan, Mount Everest, Lake Saif-
    ul-Muluk.
   Institutional nouns can change meaning: go to school = attend as a student; go to the school = go to a
    particular school building.

    Common trap: Choose a/an by pronunciation, not spelling. "University" begins with /ju:/, so "a
    university"; "hour" begins with a vowel sound, so "an hour".

4. Prepositions
Prepositions are heavily collocational. Many questions cannot be solved by literal
translation; the tested phrase must be learned as an English pattern.

What You Must Know
   Time: at 7:00; on Monday/on 14 August; in 2026/in September/in the morning; since 2022; for three years;
    by Friday = no later than Friday; until Friday = continuing up to Friday.
   Place: at a point; in an enclosed/large area; on a surface. Movement: to, into, onto, across, through, over,
    along, towards, from.
   Common adjective + preposition patterns: good at, interested in, proud of, afraid of, responsible for,
    capable of, similar to, different from.
   Common verb + preposition patterns: depend on, belong to, insist on, apply for, listen to, suffer from,
    consist of, apologize for, agree with a person / agree on a matter.
   Between is standard for two or for clearly separate individual relationships; among is used within a group.
    Beside = next to; besides = in addition to.

    Common trap: Do not add unnecessary prepositions: discuss the issue (not discuss about), enter the
    room (not enter into the room in ordinary transitive use), request something from someone.

5. Active and Passive Voice
Voice changes the focus of a sentence. Active voice makes the doer the grammatical
subject; passive voice makes the receiver of the action the subject. The basic passive
formula is appropriate form of BE + past participle.

What You Must Know
      Simple present: writes -> is/am/are written. Simple past: wrote -> was/were written.
      Present continuous: is writing -> is being written. Past continuous: was writing -> was being written.
      Present perfect: has written -> has been written. Past perfect: had written -> had been written.
      Future/modal: will write -> will be written; can/must/should write -> can/must/should be written.
      Keep tense and meaning unchanged. Move the active object to subject position. Add "by + agent" only
       when the agent is important or necessary.
      Only transitive verbs normally form passives because an object is required. Intransitive verbs such as
       arrive, sleep, die, happen usually cannot be passivized.

       Common trap: The passive auxiliary carries tense; the lexical verb remains a past participle. Do not
       confuse "is being repaired" with "is repaired" or "has been repaired".



Voice Conversion Table
    Active pattern                                         Passive pattern
    writes                                                 is/am/are written
    wrote                                                  was/were written
    is writing                                             is being written
    was writing                                            was being written
    has written                                            has been written
    had written                                            had been written
    will write                                             will be written
    can write                                              can be written

6. Direct and Indirect Speech
Reported speech changes viewpoint. You must track reporting verb, pronouns, time/place
words, tense backshift, and sentence type (statement, question, command, request,
exclamation, wish).

What You Must Know
      With a past reporting verb, common backshifts are: present simple -> past simple; present continuous ->
       past continuous; present perfect -> past perfect; past simple -> past perfect; will -> would; can -> could;
       may -> might.
      No backshift is normally necessary for universal truths, continuing facts, or when the reporting verb is
       present/future and the time reference remains valid.
      Time/place changes when perspective shifts: now -> then; today -> that day; yesterday -> the previous
       day; tomorrow -> the next day; here -> there; this -> that; these -> those.
      Statements: said (that)... / told + object (that)... Questions: asked + if/whether for yes-no questions; asked
       + wh-word for wh-questions, using statement word order.
      Commands/requests/advice: told/ordered/requested/advised + object + (not) to-infinitive. Exclamations
       often use exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise; wishes may use wished/prayed.

           Common trap: Do not keep question inversion in indirect questions. "He asked where I was going" is
           correct; "where was I going" is not.

    am/is/are                                                 was/were
    present simple                                            past simple
    present perfect                                           past perfect
    past simple                                               past perfect
    will                                                      would

7. Synonyms and Antonyms
Vocabulary matching requires meaning in context, not approximate association. A synonym
should preserve the core sense; an antonym should oppose it on the relevant semantic
dimension.

What You Must Know
   Learn common high-frequency pairs: commence/begin, terminate/end, conceal/reveal, scarce/abundant,
    rigid/flexible, hostile/friendly, superior/inferior.
   Watch degree: "annoyed" is not always equal to "furious"; "warm" is not the same as "hot." The closest
    available option is usually required.
   Use roots and affixes when possible: bene- (good), mal- (bad), anti- (against), pre- (before), post- (after),
    -less (without), -ful (full of).
   Check part of speech: if the target word is an adjective, prefer an adjective option when meanings are
    otherwise close.

    Common trap: Do not choose a word merely because it belongs to the same topic. "Obstacle" and
    "journey" may appear together, but "hindrance" is the semantic synonym of "obstacle".

8. Sentence Correction
Sentence correction combines subject-verb agreement, tense, pronouns, articles,
prepositions, modifiers, countability, parallelism, comparatives, and idiomatic
constructions.

What You Must Know
   Agreement: each/every/either/neither normally takes a singular verb; "one of + plural noun" takes a
    singular main verb; uncountable nouns such as furniture, information, advice, equipment are singular mass
    nouns.
   Auxiliaries: after do/does/did, use the base verb. After modals, use the base verb: can go, must work,
    should study.
   Gerund/infinitive patterns: enjoy doing, avoid doing, deny doing, look forward to doing, be used to doing;
    decide to do, want to do, plan to do.
   Comparisons: prefer X to Y; senior/junior/superior/inferior to; older than for general age; elder is restricted
    mainly to family relationships and not normally followed by than in standard exam usage.
   Avoid redundancy: return home, repeat, revert in context; do not say "return back" in formal exam English.
    Keep modifiers close to what they modify.

    Common trap: A sentence can sound familiar and still be wrong. Test grammar explicitly: subject,
    finite verb, agreement, tense, complement pattern, and modifier placement.



Fast Error-Detection Checklist
5. Locate the subject and finite verb. Check agreement.
6. Check tense/time words and auxiliary + verb form.
7. Check article/preposition/collocation.
8. Check countable vs uncountable noun.
9. Check pronoun reference and comparative structure.
10. Check parallelism and unnecessary repetition.

10. Sentence Completion
Sentence completion tests grammar and logic simultaneously. First determine the relation
between clauses, then select a word or structure that is grammatically compatible.

What You Must Know
   Cause/reason: because, since, as. Result: therefore, consequently, so. Contrast: but, yet, however,
    although, whereas, nevertheless.
   Condition: if, unless (= if not), provided that, in case (precaution, not the same as if). Purpose: to, in order
    to, so as to, so that.
   Correlative pairs: both...and; either...or; neither...nor; not only...but also; no sooner...than;
    hardly/scarcely...when.
   Conditional patterns: if + present, will + base; if + past, would + base; if + past perfect, would have + past
    participle.
   Parallelism matters: linked items should use matching grammatical forms.

    Common trap: Read the entire sentence before choosing. A grammatically possible option can still be
    logically wrong if the connector expresses the wrong relationship.

11. Comprehension
Comprehension questions test main idea, supporting detail, inference, vocabulary in
context, tone, purpose, and logical structure. The correct answer must be supported by the
passage, not by outside knowledge.

What You Must Know
     Main idea: ask what claim or theme organizes most of the passage. Do not choose a detail that appears
      only once.
     Detail: return to the exact sentence and verify wording. Beware options that reverse cause and effect or
      add an unsupported absolute such as always/never.
     Inference: choose what follows reasonably from the text, not what is merely possible in the real world.
     Vocabulary in context: replace the word with each option and check whether meaning and tone fit the
      sentence.
     Timed strategy: read the question stems quickly, read the passage for structure, answer direct details first,
      then inference/tone. If one question is consuming excessive time, mark and return.

      Common trap: Extreme options are often distractors, but do not reject them automatically; reject them
      because the passage does not support them.



Timed Reading Method
11. Read for structure: topic, claim, support, contrast, conclusion.
12. Answer direct detail questions before inference/tone.
13. For vocabulary-in-context, replace the word with the option and reread the sentence.
14. Reject options that add facts not in the passage.

Final Rapid-Revision Sheets
Agreement
   Each/every/either/neither -> usually singular verb.
   One of + plural noun + singular verb.
   News, furniture, information, advice, equipment -> singular/uncountable.
   Police -> plural in standard British-style usage: The police are investigating.

Tense
   Finished past time (yesterday/last week) -> simple past, not present perfect.
   Since = starting point; for = duration.
   Earlier of two past actions -> often past perfect.
   By + future deadline -> often future perfect.

Articles
   a university / a European / a one-eyed man.
   an hour / an honest man / an MBA.
   the Indus / the Arabian Sea / the Himalayas / the United Kingdom.
   No article: Pakistan, Asia, Urdu, cricket, Mount Everest.

Prepositions
   good at; interested in; proud of; afraid of; responsible for.
   depend on; belong to; insist on; consist of; apologize for.
   at 8:00; on Monday; in 2026.
   beside = next to; besides = in addition to.

Voice
   Passive = BE + V3.
   Continuous passive adds being.
   Perfect passive adds been.
   Modal passive = modal + be + V3.

Reported Speech
   said to + object -> told + object (often).
   Yes/no question -> asked if/whether.
   Wh-question -> asked + wh-word + statement order.
   Command -> told/ordered + object + to-infinitive; negative command -> not to.

Correction
   did/does/do + base verb.
   prefer X to Y; senior/superior to.
   look forward to + -ing; be used to + -ing.
   avoid redundancy: return home, discuss the issue.

Comprehension
   Answer from the passage, not outside knowledge.
   Main idea covers the whole passage, not one example.
   Inference must be supported, not merely possible.
   Watch absolute distractors: always, never, completely, only.

    Final instruction: Before the real test, revise your error log rather than rereading everything. The
    mistakes you repeatedly make are more important than the rules you already answer correctly.
